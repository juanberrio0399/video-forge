## CHAPTER 1 — Scaling the number  (0:18-1:30)
NARRATION: "Let's start small and zoom out. In 2025, YouTube pulled in more than sixty billion dollars in a single year. Do the math and that's about nineteen hundred dollars every second. Blink, and there goes a decent used car. Wait one minute, and it's over a hundred and fourteen thousand dollars — more than most people make in a year. Give it a full hour, and YouTube has made almost seven million dollars. And in one ordinary day? Around a hundred and sixty-four million dollars. That's not a good year for most companies. That's Tuesday for YouTube."
[VIS: counter animating second -> minute -> hour -> day, each with a bar]
[SHORT: "one minute = more than most people make in a year" es un Short perfecto]

## CHAPTER 2 — Where the money comes from  (1:30-3:00)
NARRATION: "So who's paying? Two big buckets. The first, and biggest, is ads — those clips before and during videos. In 2024 alone, YouTube ads brought in about thirty-six billion dollars. The fourth quarter was so strong it broke ten billion dollars in a single three-month stretch for the first time ever, partly because the US election flooded the platform with political ad money. The second bucket is subscriptions — YouTube Premium, and YouTube TV, where people pay every month to skip ads or stream live channels. Add those in, and that's how you get past sixty billion. Your attention, quietly, is the product being sold."
[VIS: two stacked bars — Ads $36B vs Subscriptions; Q4 spike highlighted]

## CHAPTER 3 — Bigger than Netflix  (3:00-4:00)
NARRATION: "Here's where it gets wild. For years, Netflix was the giant everyone watched. But in 2025, YouTube quietly passed it — YouTube now brings in more total money than Netflix does. A platform built on free videos, cat clips, and creators filming in their bedrooms is now out-earning Hollywood's favorite streaming empire. And it did it without producing almost any of the videos itself."
[VIS: YouTube bar overtaking Netflix bar, animated]
[SHORT: "YouTube passed Netflix" — reveal con las dos barras cruzandose]

## CHAPTER 4 — The part nobody expects  (4:00-5:15)
NARRATION: "Now the twist. You'd assume all this money just piles up inside Google. It doesn't. For ad revenue, YouTube keeps forty-five percent — and hands the other fifty-five percent straight to the creators who made the videos. Fifty-five percent. That means a huge slice of those billions leaves Google and lands in the pockets of people filming in spare rooms all over the world. YouTube isn't just a money machine — it's the biggest paycheck engine the internet has ever built for regular people. Every second that counter ticks, part of it is going to someone's rent, someone's camera, someone's dream."
[VIS: the money river splitting — 45% to Google, 55% to creators]
